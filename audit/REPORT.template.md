## 3. Item 1: overlap

**Method.** For each analysis the propensity score by arm, the share of rows outside [0.05, 0.95], the AUC of the score, a calendar-month positivity table, and the estimate after trimming to the band. PSM scores are the audited in-sample logistic scores. DML scores are the audited cross-fitted out-of-fold scores, for both learners. Trimming has two variants for PSM (drop rows and keep the original score; drop rows and refit score and matching) and two for DML (drop rows; clip the score to the band, as DoubleML's trimming does).

{{fig:overlap_ps_by_arm.png|Propensity score by arm for each audited analysis. Left: full [0,1] axis (log counts) with the 0.05 / 0.95 band. Right: the same scores zoomed to their range.}}

{{include:results/01_overlap.md}}

**Reading.**

- **PSM v2 and DML-ridge pass the band check vacuously.** All scores sit in {{ov_v2_range}} (v2) and {{ov_ridge_range}} (ridge). The AUC is {{ov_v2_auc}} and {{ov_ridge_auc}}: the covariates carry essentially no information about morning versus evening, so the score is close to the base rate for every row. A band that spans 0.05 to 0.95 cannot flag a problem in a score that never leaves 0.06-0.12.
- **PSM Phase 3.** {{ov_p3_out}} rows ({{ov_p3_pct}}%) are below 0.05, all evening rows. All {{ov_p3_deck0}} of them are rows with `deck_enc == 0`, a covariate for which no morning row exists. Dropping them and keeping the original score leaves the estimate at {{p3_keep_pp}} pp; refitting the score on the remaining rows moves the matched estimate to {{p3_refit_pp}} pp. That shift is {{p3_shift_sd}} standard deviations of the estimator's shuffled-label null ({{p3_null_sd_pp}} pp, §4) and stays inside the notebook's reported 95% interval [-0.65, +0.51] pp.
- **DML with gradient boosting.** {{ov_gbm_n}} rows ({{ov_gbm_pct}}%) have an out-of-fold score below 0.05, none above 0.95. The boosting learner picks up calendar structure (AUC {{ov_gbm_auc}}): in the {{ov_gbm_zero}} of {{ov_gbm_months}} months with no morning row its mean score is {{ov_gbm_ps_zero}}, against {{ov_gbm_ps_nonzero}} elsewhere. Dropping those rows changes the estimate from +{{s0_gbm}} to +{{trim_gbm_drop}} ms and clipping gives +{{trim_gbm_clip}} ms.
- **Positivity by calendar month does not hold in the audited samples.** In PSM v2, {{ov_v2_zero}} of {{ov_v2_months}} calendar months contain no morning row, yet the model's mean score in those months is {{ov_v2_ps_zero}} (against {{ov_v2_ps_nonzero}} in months that do have morning rows): the linear logistic score cannot represent "no morning reviews before May 2025". The notebook's own limitation note says the same in words.
- **PSM v1** (not re-run, §9) reports in its saved output the same shape: scores {{v1_ps_range}}, train accuracy {{v1_acc}}, no unit outside the band.
- **With the clock read in Eastern time** (§5.2) the picture is different: {{ov_east_morning}} of {{ov_east_n}} PSM v2 rows are morning, scores span {{ov_east_range}}, and no calendar month is empty.

{{fig:overlap_month_positivity.png|PSM v2: share of rows that are morning in each calendar month (bars) and the mean propensity score the model assigns (line), UTC clock as audited (left) and Eastern clock (right).}}

**Does trimming change the estimate?** For PSM v2, DML-ridge and DML-GBM: no (table above). For Phase 3: removing 27 rows and refitting moves it by about 0.5 pp.

## 4. Item 2: placebo checks

### 4.1 Method

- **(a) Shuffle the labels.** The morning/evening labels are shuffled {{B}} times and the whole estimator is refit each time (PSM: new propensity model and new matching; DML: new propensity nuisance). Two shuffles are run: unrestricted, and within calendar month, which keeps the clustering of morning reviews in time. For DML the outcome nuisance never sees the label, so it is fit once and only the propensity nuisance is refit per shuffle; this is exactly equivalent to rerunning the pipeline.
- **(b) Pre-treatment outcome.** The same card's previous review (its response time with capped 60-second values excluded, or whether it was retained for Phase 3). The current review's time of day cannot cause it.
- **(c) Outcomes the treatment cannot affect.** The kanji count of the card's Japanese field and the note's position in the deck (rank of note id). Both are fixed before any review.

**Phase 3 is different and needs care.** The raw matched difference in kanji count between morning and evening rows is {{p3_kanji}} (analytic p = {{p3_kanji_p}}) and in deck position {{p3_pos}} (p = {{p3_pos_p}}), which would read as strong imbalance on card content. But the same estimator applied to *shuffled* labels is not centred on zero for those outcomes: its null mean is {{p3_kanji_nullmean}} for kanji and {{p3_pos_nullmean}} for deck position. Against that null the real values are ordinary (centred p = {{p3_kanji_pc}} and {{p3_pos_pc}}). In other words a difference of this size is what the matcher produces under shuffled labels, so the raw difference says little about the labels. The analytic p-values above are not meaningful for this estimator.

### 5.2 The clock

`pd.to_datetime(revlog['id'], unit='ms')` is UTC. Every audited analysis takes `hour` from it. Two independent sources place the learner at UTC-5 (US Eastern): the machine's time zone, and `col.crt` = {{crt_utc}} UTC, which is 04:00 at UTC-5 (Anki's default day rollover).

{{fig:leakage_timing.png|Left and centre: change in revlog.factor against the previous row of the same card, grouped by this row's button and by the previous row's button. ### 5.5 Cross-fitting folds, scalers and time order

{{include:results/03_C.md}}

- **Row-level folds split cards and notes.** Each card has {{reviews_per_card}} reviews on average and every note has {{cards_per_note}} cards. In the five held-out folds {{fold_card}}% of rows have a review of the same card in training, {{fold_note}}% have a review of the same note, and {{fold_later}}% have a *later* review of the same card in training (ranges across folds). This check shows the estimate depends on that; it does not say which design is right.
- **Scalers and encoders.** Scalers are fit on all rows in v2, Phase 3, v1 and DML (`dashboard.py` lines 202-203, before folding). - **The v1 to v2 change predates the repository.** The v1 notebook, the v2 notebook and the main notebook were all added in one commit (`5a967f2`, 2026-04-24 13:59 -0400) and never touched again. Their file times (2026-04-23 15:14 for v1, 15:37 for v2) show v2 was written 23 minutes after v1 the day before. No commit contains a diff of the change.
- **What changed, from the v2 notebook's own markdown.** Hard-card definition: from `cards.factor <= 2.65` (the lowest quartile of *today's* ease factors, 2,742 cards) to `revlog.factor < 2.5` on each review row; a `month_idx` covariate was added; hard reviews fell from 7,592 to 4,252; the share of reviews from 2025 fell from about 82% to about 72%. **Why:** v1 selected cards on their end state, and those cards had most of their reviews in early 2025, so the morning/evening comparison was confounded with the learning curve.

- **What this audit adds.** The v2 fix replaced a today's-state selection with a per-row selection, but `revlog.factor` is a post-answer value (§5.3), so the replacement is not point-in-time; and the v2 notebook's note "Zero morning hard reviews in Jan-Apr 2025 and Oct 2025" is about UTC hours (§5.2).
- The word "leakage" for the v1/v2 change appears in this repo's history nowhere; the nearest repo text is "current snapshot" and "at review time" in the v2 notebook. The sentence "Corrected snapshot-vs-review-time feature leakage between v1 and v2" appears only in a resume draft written earlier in this working session, not in the repo.

- **The unit of assignment.** Assignment was by note (three cards per note); the z-test and the Mann-Whitney test treat reviews as independent. Clustering by note moves the lapse p from {{rct_z_p}} to {{rct_cl_p}} and the log-response-time p to {{rct_cl_rt_p}}. A re-randomisation test over notes on the *reported* response-time statistic (pooled median difference) gives p={{rct_ri_med_p}}, against Mann-Whitney p={{rct_mw_p}}.

The reported post-period gap is +{{rct_diff}} pp. The difference between the two gaps is {{rct_did}} pp (p={{rct_did_p}}). The pre-experiment median response-time gap is +{{pre_rt}} ms (p={{pre_rt_p}}).
- **Cannot-be-affected outcomes.** Kanji count and deck position of the notes do not differ by arm (re-randomisation p={{rct_kanji_p}} and {{rct_pos_p}}).

## 9. What I could not verify

- **Whether the read-aloud happened.** No record in the collection tables or the repo. The media folder was examined in an earlier session, not in this audit.
- **The learner's time zone for every review.** Eastern is inferred from the machine time zone and `col.crt` (collection creation). Travel or a change of time zone would not show up in either. The Eastern runs use the `America/New_York` zone including daylight saving.
- **PSM v1.** Not re-run: v1 selects cards by today's `cards.factor`, and the collection's `cards.factor` has changed since 2026-04-23. Its overlap is read from the executed notebook output only.
- **The Cox numbers as published.** Reconstructed from `revlog` ({{surv_cards}} cards, {{surv_events}} events against {{nb_cards}} and {{nb_events}}). The original card table is gone.
- **The ITS.** Read and listed in §5.1; not re-estimated.
- **The deployed app.** I did not open the Streamlit Cloud app. That it runs the Cloud branch is inferred from `CLOUD_MODE = not DB_PATH.exists()` and the committed `data/revlog.parquet` (last review {{cloud_last}}). The figures at that URL may differ from `results/06_cloud_branch.md` if the app has not redeployed.
- **The DML run through Streamlit.** The audit re-implements `run_dml` in `audit_lib.py`; it matches the July run to the printed precision, but the dashboard function itself was not executed under Streamlit.
- **Why the Phase 3 matcher is off-centre** under shuffled labels (§4.4). Row order and tie-breaking are ruled out; the cause is not established.
- **Which DML fold design is right** (§5.5). The checks show the estimate depends on it.
- **The sensitivity specifications** (§5.6) change both the sample and the covariates; they say how fragile an estimate is, not what the true effect is.
